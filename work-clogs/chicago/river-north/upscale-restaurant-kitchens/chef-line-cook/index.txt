<h2 id="the-chefs-perspective">The chef&#39;s perspective</h2>
<p>A chef&#39;s day can stretch from early prep and ordering through the last plates of service. Time is split between the pass, the prep tables, the walk-in cooler, and the office, and a lot of it is spent standing or walking.</p>
<p>Moving around the whole kitchen means crossing every kind of floor it has: tile near the dish area that can be wet, spots near fryers and grills that can be greasy, and cold zones by refrigeration. Kitchens are hot, aisles can be narrow, and people pass each other quickly with pans and knives in hand.</p>
<p>If the kitchen has a footwear rule, the chef may be the person who explains it to new staff as well as the person who follows it.</p>

<h2 id="the-line-cooks-perspective">The line cook&#39;s perspective</h2>
<p>On the line, the work happens at one station: grill, sauté, fry, or garde manger. A line cook can spend most of a shift in that small space, turning between the range, the cutting board, and the reach-in, then speeding up when tickets stack up.</p>
<p>The floor at a station catches splashes of water, oil, and sauce during service, and prep and cleanup add more time on damp tile. Stations near the range and fryer run hot, and cooks work close together.</p>
<p>If the kitchen has a footwear rule, it applies on the line too, so a line cook&#39;s checklist starts in the same place as the chef&#39;s.</p>

<h2 id="what-to-look-for-in-kitchen-clogs-for-the-line-and-the-pass">What to look for in kitchen clogs for the line and the pass</h2>
<p>Use these as buying criteria when you compare listings. Start with your own workplace&#39;s footwear requirements.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Slip resistance.</strong> Look for a listing that says slip-resistant and, ideally, names the slip test or result your employer asks for. One test you may see is ASTM F2913. ASTM describes it as a lab test method that measures friction between a shoe and a floor to evaluate relative slip performance (<a href="https://www.astm.org/f2913-19.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ASTM</a>). Naming the test tells you how grip was measured, not that a shoe can&#39;t slip. For examples of clogs whose listings describe slip-resistant soles, see our <a href="/livysclogs-preview/dansko-clogs-guide/">Dansko clog guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Closed toe and heel.</strong> A fully enclosed upper keeps spills off the top of your foot. If your job needs toe protection, look for a listing that states a safety-toe rating.</li>
<li><strong>Easy to clean.</strong> Uppers that wipe down or rinse off are simpler to keep up with than fabric that absorbs spills.</li>
<li><strong>Support for long hours standing.</strong> Look for a supportive footbed, a firm heel counter, and a sole shape (such as a rocker bottom) that the brand says is designed for standing and walking. Our <a href="/livysclogs-preview/dansko-professional-review/">Dansko Professional review</a> explains how one rocker-bottom clog is meant to work.</li>
<li><strong>Fit.</strong> Clogs fit differently from sneakers, and sizing varies by brand. Read each brand&#39;s size guide before ordering.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Livy&#39;s Clogs isn&#39;t a medical provider. If you have foot pain or a condition like plantar fasciitis, talk with a podiatrist.</em></p>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
<p><strong>Do chefs and line cooks need the same kind of shoe?</strong>
The jobs differ, but the floor is shared. The wet, greasy, and hot spots a chef crosses are the same ones a line cook works beside, so the buying criteria above are the same for both roles.</p>
<p><strong>What does &quot;slip-resistant&quot; mean on a listing?</strong>
It means the maker says the sole is designed to grip on slick surfaces. Some listings also name a lab test, such as ASTM F2913, that measures grip. If your employer asks for a specific test or result, look for that exact wording or ask the brand.</p>
<p><strong>Can I wear clogs with vent holes in a kitchen?</strong>
Vent holes leave open gaps in the top of the shoe. If your workplace has a footwear rule, check whether it allows them before you buy. Our <a href="/livysclogs-preview/crocs-clogs-guide/">Crocs clog guide</a> notes which styles are closed.</p>
<p><strong>How should a work clog fit?</strong>
It depends on the brand. Some work clogs are meant to let your heel lift slightly as you walk. Read the brand&#39;s fit guide and the listing&#39;s sizing notes.</p>
